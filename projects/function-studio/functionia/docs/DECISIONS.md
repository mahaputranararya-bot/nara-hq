# Functionia — Decisions made during the build

The brief said: when something is unclear, make the most reasonable choice and write it down. Here is every such choice.

## Project setup
1. **Where the game lives.** The brief assumed an empty folder. The game is in `projects/function-studio/functionia/` inside Nara's office repo, so it sits next to the other Function Studio files. The repo already used git, so there was no `git init`. Commits were grouped per milestone group, not one per milestone.
2. **Who built it.** The office's School CEO agent has no shell access, so it couldn't run the tests. Azalea (the front-desk agent) built the game directly and kept `projects/function-studio/status.md` and `board.md` up to date.
3. **Script loading.** Classic `<script>` tags in a fixed order with one global `window.F`. Every pure-math file also exports for Node (a small UMD wrapper), so the same code runs in the game and in the tests. No build step: `index.html` opens by double-click.
4. **Rendering.** A hybrid design. One full-screen Canvas 2D draws the world (backgrounds, graphs, Ori, Delta, effects). All important text, inputs and buttons are real HTML on top, which is better for accessibility and screen readers.
5. **Art in separate modules.** `js/engine/art.js` (scenery), `portraits.js` (characters) and the `drawBody` methods in `ori.js` and `delta.js` hold all the drawing, so Kayyira can replace the art without touching game logic.

## Math input and checking
6. **Parser.** A small recursive-descent parser, with no `eval` or `Function`. It accepts integers, decimals (`.5`, and `0,5` with a decimal comma, which is common in Indonesia), fractions, negatives with any minus sign (−, -, –), `sqrt()`/`√`, `+ − × ÷ ^` and brackets. It also allows a leading `x =` or `m =`. Values are kept as exact fractions whenever possible.
7. **Tolerance.** Whole-number answers must match exactly (`19.0` is fine, `19.001` is not). Other answers accept the exact fraction, or a decimal strictly within 0.01 (so `0.33` counts for ⅓, but `0.49` does not count for ½).
8. **Inequality answers** ("choose any b with b ≤ ½") are checked exactly, using fractions, with no tolerance at the boundary. This keeps "at most" and "below" truly different, as the brief asks.
9. **Dividing by zero and square roots of negatives** typed into any box trigger a friendly "Break the Model" glitch (it counts as a try) instead of an error.
10. **Every generator checks its own answer key** by substituting it back before showing the puzzle. Tests run 500 seeds per type (15,000 puzzles).

## Game structure
11. **Unlocks.** Mirrorvale opens after the opening. Practice opens after Learn, the Trial after one Practice round, and the Glitch Zone after Learn. Lineara, Parabolis, Cubrion and Expona open after Mirrorvale, in any order, with the brief's recommended order shown as a "Suggested" flag. Rationalis opens when those five are restored.
12. **Practice** is 5 puzzles per round, one of each puzzle type. The brief allowed 5–6; 5 matches the 5 types. Replays give a fresh round. The best stars for each of the 5 slots count towards the star total. Practice Mode (endless) shows stars but does not add to the total, so the total can't be farmed.
13. **Stars.** 3 ★ = no hints and correct within 2 tries. 2 ★ = at most 1 hint level and at most 4 tries. 1 ★ = anything else, including "Show me the answer". Speed never matters.
14. **Hint timing.** Learn: hints at once. Practice, Puzzle of the Day and challenges: after 1 wrong try. Trial and Final Battle: after 2 wrong tries. After 3 misses Delta *offers* a hint, but never forces it.
15. **Predict first.** Trial, Final Battle and Class Challenge have no live preview and no sliders. Many "read the equation" puzzles keep the graph hidden until Launch (vertex, roots, end behaviour, y-intercept, walls), so the player predicts and then tests.
16. **Rationalis and the final boss.** The Warden's Trial (3 puzzles) frees the Warden and lights the 6th ring segment. The Final Battle then has 6 phases, one per kingdom skill. All five other kingdoms are required to reach Rationalis, so all 6 phases always run.
17. **Learn demos** reuse the real puzzle builder with different "sibling" numbers, then auto-play the correct launch while Delta explains. The demos are guaranteed correct and tested.
18. **The opening bridge** shows the bridge planks pointing to the answer. It is the guided first win (rubric B9), so it is meant to be easy and satisfying.
19. **Machine Pipes on a miss** shows the middle value (g(3) = 9) but not the final output. This teaches "inside first" without giving away the answer.

## Rewards and meta systems
20. **Golden puzzles.** 1 in 8, fixed by the challenge code, so a classmate gets the same golden puzzle. Rewards alternate between a fun fact card and a golden-only cosmetic, all purely visual.
21. **Fun facts.** 17 cards. Each restored kingdom gives one of its cards, and golden puzzles give the rest. Every number is checked in `tests/content.test.js`.
22. **Challenge codes** look like `PAR-3-8K2Q`: kingdom, puzzle type, and a 4-character Crockford base-32 seed (about 1 million puzzles per type). Typing is forgiving: lower case, spaces, and O/I/L read as 0/1/1.
23. **Puzzle of the Day** uses the computer's local date. It picks from restored kingdoms only, so the same date with the same kingdoms gives the same puzzle. Solving it stamps the Journal calendar. There are no streaks.
24. **Day/night and the seed** follow progress, not real time. The sky goes from glitch night (0 kingdoms) to dawn (3) to day (6). The Expona seed appears once Expona is restored and grows with the number of restored kingdoms.
25. **Kingdom gifts are visible.** Mirrorvale draws a faint y = x line on the map, and each gift is named on the Restored screen.
26. **Ruler callbacks** play when you return to a restored kingdom from the map. "Talk to the ruler" replays them.

## Feel, safety and accessibility
27. **Flash safety.** The screen-tear pattern changes at most about 3 times per second (every 340 ms) and eases between changes. The Glitch's body jitters at 3 Hz, and there are no full-screen bright flashes. Reduced motion (switched on automatically from the operating system) replaces all of this with a soft colour pulse.
28. **Keyboard.** R and H call `preventDefault()`, so the letter is never typed into an answer box. H works even while typing, because "h" never appears in a number answer. Enter in a box launches.
29. **Resetting a save** needs two steps ("Reset…" then "Yes, reset it"), as the brief asks. The game has no other confirmation prompts and no guilt messages.
30. **Music** comes from a small procedural sequencer: one theme per kingdom plus title, map, night, boss and ending themes. It is ducked to 40% under dialogue.
31. **Toasts** never pile up: at most 2 show at once, and several unlocks at once become one message.

## Testing
32. **Dev tools only:** Playwright scripts in `tests/e2e/` (smoke, tour, late game, accessibility, performance). The game itself has zero dependencies.
33. **The f(x)plained Short URLs** start empty, as the brief says, and show "Coming soon on f(x)plained!".

## v1.2 (after Nara's play-through: "the UI collides, the characters are simple, improve the sound")
34. **Art stays code-drawn**, as Nara chose: offline, tiny, and swappable by Kayyira. A shared painting toolkit (`js/engine/paint.js`) gives every character shading, rim light, gloss, expressive eyes with lids, brows and mouths. Ori and Delta were redrawn, and each ruler is now a layered half-body portrait with a worried (corrupted) and a happy (restored) mood. The dev panel has an **Art gallery** that shows them all.
35. **Puzzle panel.** Answer boxes became cards with the label above the box. The inverse puzzle has its own inline formula row. Launch has a full row, with Retry and Hint sharing the next. Long choice options go one per row. A Playwright audit (`tests/e2e/layout.js`) checks 2,064 panel states at 1366×768, 1280×720 and 1093×540 (a 1366×768 Windows laptop at 125% zoom), in normal and Large text, and fails on any overlap or overflow.
36. **Scenes.** Every kingdom gets ambient particles (snow sparkles, pollen, embers, fireflies, spores, static), fog, light rays and a vignette, all computed from time with no stored state. Map islands are floating rocks with cliff strata that glow when restored, and the Origin is a carved plinth. The graph card is frosted, and graph labels move out of the way of Ori's coordinate badge.
37. **Sound** is still 100% Web Audio, as Nara chose. The engine has synth instruments (pad, pluck, bell, marimba, bass, lead, kick/snare/hat/shaker), a code-generated convolution reverb and a tempo echo. 12 composed themes (A A B A form, real seventh-chord progressions) each quote the "Ori motif". Themes crossfade over 1.5 s. The map theme adds layers as kingdoms are restored. Sound effects are layered, the star sound rises with each star, and the glitch is bit-crushed. Each character has its own dialogue voice blips.
38. **Checking sound without speakers:** `tests/e2e/audio.js` renders every theme offline. It checks none is silent and none clips (peaks about 0.3), and saves WAV previews.

## v1.3–v1.4 (Nara: "make it way more beautiful, still all in code; no need to stay under 2 MB")
39. **Size cap lifted, offline kept.** Nara agreed the 2 MB limit was only a guideline. Everything is still drawn and composed in code and works by double-clicking `index.html`; the game is now about 0.8 MB.
40. **Painted scenes.** `js/engine/scenekit.js` holds shared tools: seeded noise, faceted crystal mountains, crystal shards, glow sprites, and cached sharp layers. Mirrorvale (`js/engine/scenes/mirrorvale.js`) has aurora, a moon, a crystal palace and a real mirror lake (the upper scene flipped and rippled). Other kingdoms keep their v1.2 scenes until they are repainted.
41. **GPU glow** (`js/engine/glfx.js`, WebGL): soft bloom, colour grade, vignette and dithering. Graph cards are masked so the maths stays crisp. It switches itself off on software-only graphics or when frames are slow, and can be turned off in Settings → Glow & lighting. Nara asked for less glow after the first preview, so it was halved.
42. **Characters.** The Mirror Queen is a full illustration (`js/engine/characters/queen.js`); Nara kept the A-line gown, and a classmate asked for her to be taller, so her gown is longer and she is drawn larger in the hub. Ori is now a star-spirit with anime eyes and a floating star.
43. **Title screen** (`js/engine/scenes/title.js`): a nebula sky, the six rules as constellations, six painted islands joined to the Origin by light, and a code-drawn gold logo. The i's are drawn as shapes, so their glowing dots always line up, whatever font the computer has. A 3-second reveal plays once per session and any key or click skips it.
44. **The opening as a short film** (`js/ui/opening.js`): one shot per narration line, with letterbox bars and subtitles. The story text is unchanged, so it still passes the word-limit test. "Skip the intro" and resuming at a saved beat still work, and the bridge puzzle (rubric B9) is unchanged.
45. **Realm Map** (`js/engine/scenes/realm.js`, `islands.js`): the sky follows progress (glitch night → dawn → day), with painted islands, light-bridge paths and a camera that glides toward the chosen island. On short screens Ori's (0, 0) badge hides so it never covers a label.
46. **Music:** a Karplus–Strong harp, FM music box, flute, formant choir and strings. Hand-arranged pieces: the main title theme, the Mirror Waltz (3/4), and a film score (prologue → omen → silence → the spark).
47. **GUI:** one frosted-glass design layer at the end of `css/style.css`: gold "gem" buttons with a light sweep, glass HUD, plaque map labels, a storybook dialogue box and a softer screen dip. Puzzle-panel sizes are unchanged, and the layout audit still reports 0 overlaps.

## v1.5 (Nara: "longer credits with our names; the film feels too fast; the islands and kingdoms are too basic; cooler transitions"; then "the shapes are too clear and sharp")
48. **Crew credits.** About 9 s before the title, once per launch, and any key or click skips it. "Group 5 presents", then three points are plotted on graph paper: **Nara**, **Uno** and **Kayyira** with their roles. They join into a triangle and fold into the Origin spark.
49. **Slower film.** The story words are unchanged. Each shot now holds long enough to read (at least 7.5 s), the six-kingdom montage lasts about 14.5 s, and captions fade in as whole lines. The film runs about 49 s. Next and Skip still work.
50. **Transitions** (`js/engine/transition.js`, drawn above everything): "begin the journey" (sparkles swirl into the Origin and gold light floods the screen) and a kingdom portal (a tunnel in the kingdom's colour with its own curve spinning inside), plus the reverse back to the map. With reduced motion, the plain fade is used.
51. **Painterly rendering** (`js/engine/painter.js`). Every still scene layer is repainted from brush strokes after Hertzmann's method: coarse to fine; colours taken from the clean picture; strokes following edges; finer brushes only where there is detail; kept inside the picture's own silhouette; with paper grain on top. Painting runs a few milliseconds per frame in the background: the clean layer shows first, then the painting fades in (about 1.5–2 s per kingdom). Natural shapes use organic helpers (`K.blob`, `K.organic`, `K.puffCloud`, `K.foliage`, `K.dab` in `scenekit.js`) instead of perfect circles and rectangles. UI, graphs, Ori, Delta and the rulers stay crisp.
52. **All six kingdoms painted** (`js/engine/scenes/kingdoms.js` + `mirrorvale.js`). Each shows its function in the landscape: Lineara (straight fields to one vanishing point), Parabolis (parabola peaks, arrows flying parabolas), Cubrion (a river winding like a cubic), Expona (leaf rows 1.6 times bigger each row), Rationalis (vertical and horizontal asymptote walls that pulses never cross).
53. **Island dioramas** on the map: two-tier rocks, cloud collars, detailed landmarks and small animations. The map islands are bigger, and their labels move to the side where they would cover another island.
54. **Five new rulers** (`js/engine/characters/rulers.js`, built from shared parts in `body.js`), drawn in the Mirror Queen's style with worried and happy moods. Each ruler's prop shows their function: the King's ruler-sceptre, the Archer's parabola bow, the Serpent's cubic body, the Witch's blooming torch, and the Warden's dashed asymptote staff.
55. **Sound from the first second.** Browsers block sound until the player clicks or presses a key, so the title now opens on a "Click to begin" screen. That click starts the music, then the crew credits play (now 5.6 s instead of about 9 s).
56. **Hold to skip.** The credits and the opening film use a skip button that must be held for about 0.9 s while a ring fills in (or hold Esc). A quick click never skips by accident. In the film, a single click only moves to the next caption once it has been on screen for 1.2 s.
57. **Real dissolves in the film.** Each kingdom card in the montage is drawn into its own picture first, then the whole picture fades into the next, so nothing (lines, grid, titles) is left behind. Each kingdom's rule draws itself across the sky in light, then fades.
58. **A waterfall that flows.** Cubrion's waterfall is a curved sheet built from a streak texture that speeds up as it falls (gravity), with soft edges, foam at the lip, a churning pool, broken ripples, rising mist and spray. The same waterfall pours off the Cubrion island on the map.
59. **Characters lit like the scene** (`js/engine/charfx.js`). Every ruler, Ori and Delta in a kingdom gets that kingdom's light: shade on the side away from the sun or moon, its colour on the lit side, a rim of light on the edge, brush grain so they read as paint, and a soft shadow on the stage. Dialogue portraits get the same light. The look is baked into one sheet per size, so it costs one extra pass per frame.
60. **A better glitch.** Corrupted rulers no longer have straight lines drawn over them. The glitch lives inside the figure: faint scanlines and colour fringes all the time, and every few seconds a short burst where slices slip sideways and blocks break off. The "You broke the model!" screen effect now tears into red and cyan channels, copies blocks to the wrong place, and rolls a tracking band down the screen. Corrupted kingdoms have small fragments of broken data drifting in the air. Still flash-safe: patterns change at most 3 times a second, nothing flashes, and reduced motion turns it off.
61. **Depth in the kingdoms.** The layer nearest the camera is slightly out of focus; aerial haze sits between the far hills and the middle ground; out-of-focus specks of light drift in front of everyone (kept faint around the ruler); and the camera drifts slowly so the layers slide past each other. Reduced motion slows the drift and specks.
